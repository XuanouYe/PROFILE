At Shanghai Grand Theatre, Bayerische Staatsoper’s 2006 production directed by Peter Konwitschny. It is already quite abstract in Act I: Senta, who might be expected to function as a MacGuffin, drifts ghostlike across the stage, silently giving the most conspicuous performance in passages otherwise occupied only by baritones and basses. This time I noticed a very funny detail in Act II. When the tenor Erik accuses Senta of believing in a “portrait,” the smashed portrait of the Dutchman is actually somewhere out of sight. Meanwhile, nobody notices the image of the muscular man in the gym backdrop silently watching everything unfold. The final explosion and silence extend the production’s earlier moments of manufactured “astonishment,” becoming a symbol of redemption. Konwitschny’s taste of twenty years ago still works today, perhaps because neither in Munich then nor in Shanghai now is anyone willing to believe that the ghostly sailors and Senta can ascend to heaven at the end.
