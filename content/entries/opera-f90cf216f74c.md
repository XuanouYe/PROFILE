At the Salzburger Festspiele, Gabriela Carrizo’s new 2026 production. It offers a way of integrating dancers fully into the chorus’s stage movement. This dancing has nothing to do with Romani “exoticism”: bodies and the material of the stage directly construct a violent world—the patriarchal order of the military and the family—and show how that world produces José and his motive for murder. What is frightening is the production’s ability to combine intensely symbolic imagery with a surreal state in the ensemble passages, where dancers and opera singers act as one another’s doubles: outward performances of different aspects of the same identity. Quite extraordinary. In Act I, the successive entrances of soldiers and male and female prisoners demonstrate the command of stage movement. Act II’s bloody Dionysian dance brings a fierce acceleration; in Act III the symbolic language begins to repeat and accumulate; Act IV returns to spare, extreme metaphor. The progression is unmistakable.
