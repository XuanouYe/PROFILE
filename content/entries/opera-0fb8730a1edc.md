At Bayerische Staatsoper, Tobias Kratzer’s 2026 production. I saw his Rheingold the year before last and liked it, but just missed this new Walküre; now I have finally caught it. The Ride in Act III is filmed as the Valkyries transport corpses to the opera house—essentially what the text describes, carrying the souls of the dead to Valhalla. So the Valhalla that Hunding went to such trouble to build, borrowing money and stealing gold, turns out to be the opera house that the Bavarian government lacked the budget to finish despite years of construction. Quite funny, especially in the very space occupied by the audience. This kind of joke is Kratzer’s stock-in-trade, with a spoof of Coppola thrown in. The subsequent expansion of that space is more limited, like staging a centuries-old opera in this six-hundred-year-old opera house. Overall, it feels as though the Valhalla that fully opened up the stage in the earlier production has been reduced to a tabletop ornament in Act I. It wants to establish a stage always waiting for its audience, deliberately cultivating an apparently casual ease. In fact, most of the designs simply put whatever is being sung directly onstage; the black-and-white images of memory are rather clumsy too. Taken together, though, they become a spirit of self-mockery.
