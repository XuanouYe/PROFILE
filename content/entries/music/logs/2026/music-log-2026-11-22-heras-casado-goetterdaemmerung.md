As with _Siegfried_, the strike meant that the second run of _Götterdämmerung_ went ahead as a costumed concert performance, without the sets. The Play stream used a recording from the first run. It felt like getting to see two unrepeatable versions of Casado's Ring back to back. The two runs drew on two orchestral lineups, one for the first two operas and one for the last two. Casado's pacing was remarkably smooth. The prelude to _Das Rheingold_ is built on a sustained E-flat major harmony. The double basses open, a horn introduces the arpeggio in bar 17, and the texture thickens as the other horns enter. The music moves forward through successive entries, an expanding register and increasingly fine rhythmic subdivisions. Casado let the sound grow unhurriedly, blending the parts with a fine sense of proportion. The accents picked out individual waves within the long buildup to the Rhinemaidens' first entrance, and the layers of brass gave the climax a ceremonial character. Paris had been working on _Das Rheingold_ for two years, and Heras-Casado had also conducted it at the Vienna State Opera that summer. By this run of the _Ring_, the orchestra's playing was faultless.

In _Die Walküre_, from the dialogue to the moment Nothung is drawn, the quiet playing was transparent and the more turbulent passages surged forward. In _Siegfried_, the blows of the hammer, the singing and the force of the orchestra all registered vividly in the forging scene. In the Forest Murmurs, the strings, the woodwind birdsong and Siegfried's solo horn seemed to come from different distances, so precisely were the layers judged. "Heil dir, Sonne! Heil dir, Licht!" was especially finely shaded. The individual lines stayed distinct as the violins' brightness grew from a delicate shimmer into something intensely expressive. The orchestra's attention to sonority paid off at both ends of the dynamic range, though it briefly covered the singers a few times. Siegfried's Funeral March in _Götterdämmerung_ erupted with tremendous force. This was the first part of the _Ring_ that Wagner drafted, and one of the conductor's tasks throughout the opera is to shape how the motifs return. Heras-Casado made finely judged changes at each return, varying the colour and how closely the layers of the orchestral texture blended.
