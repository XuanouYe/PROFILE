At Staatsoper Unter den Linden, the premiere of Robert Carsen’s new 2025 production. Finally, a chance to hear Simon Rattle conduct Janáček. A very funny work in two acts: after a trip to the moon and a journey into the past, all its satire and mockery dissolve like foam in the clinking of beer glasses. Archival footage fills the gaps between fantasy and reality, while the rapid collisions of ice hockey stand in for struggle and resistance. The strange-looking inhabitants of the moon and the rebels share synchronized, unsettling movements. Yet the settings of history and the future place less emphasis on spatial dislocation; here, the illusion of Czech identity unexpectedly acquires real force. The characters largely speak in comic recitative, like an absurd fable that is then dismantled—just right for Janáček’s dissonant music, so full of imitated speech inflections.
