The production is less visually extravagant than the reviews suggest. A prison-like, geometrically divided set in muted colours. Lucia’s red clothing and the projected red footage are among the few elements using an outward, highly saturated red, corresponding to the bloodshed in the later acts. Lucia is a figure whose voice, speech and body are continually interpreted, distorted or taken over by men. Apart from the moment when forced resistance turns into a stabbing, she has little agency. This is simply juxtaposed with acts and declarations concerning real gendered violence in contemporary feminist footage. I did not find, as some reviews suggested, that one side overwhelmed the other. Instead, the opera’s implicit power relationships were effectively externalized and politicized.
