At Bayerische Staatsoper, Tobias Kratzer’s new 2024 production. The subversive invasion of modern spectacle kills off the ghost of a humanist reading of the premodern gods. Structurally akin to Dumont’s The Empire, the gods’ faces become abstract carriers of issues and symbols, punctuated by brief, dangerous Dadaist humour: the journey becomes a short film moving across the curtain; Alberich, transformed into a toad, is stopped at airport security and then stripped naked. At the opening, the Rhinemaidens’ play becomes a precocious ritual of modern leisure. Seeing “Gott ist tot” written in blood on the set, we reread Wagner once more through Nietzsche: naked animality, and the building site of Valhalla, where gods argue on foam mats and scaffolding, reaching the upper levels of the stage through the defining human activity of “making and using tools.” Once the finished Valhalla is entered, like a golden altar, they become comic, pictorial figures in the stage background.
