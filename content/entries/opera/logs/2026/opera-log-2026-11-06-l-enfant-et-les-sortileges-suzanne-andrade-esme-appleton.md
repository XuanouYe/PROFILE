Ravel's straightforward musical drama is transformed by the theatre company 1927 into a psychological world with a will of its own, full of both humour and menace. Abandoning conventional stage perspective, the production combines the visual language of silent cinema and two-dimensional animation, using projections as its primary means of storytelling. The most immediate thematic link between _Petrushka_ and _L'Enfant et les Sortilèges_ lies in the shifting relationship between the individual and an external world governed by rules. In _Petrushka_, a puppet - an "object" - longs to become a free and autonomous subject; in _L'Enfant et les Sortilèges_, a child discovers that all "objects" can be subjects in their own right. Yet a similarly cartoonish and satirical visual language could just as well be achieved through conventional stagecraft. Beyond the potential savings in production costs and the reduced complexity of directing performers, I see little artistic necessity for rendering the production in animated form.
