Finally watched Serebrennikov's *Das Rheingold* on the very last day before it disappeared from ARTE's streaming service. Well, I guess I can cross Salzburg off my plans for next year.

The production follows a rather simple concept: a post-apocalyptic Africa, with black volcanic landscapes, ice, and the ruins of civilization. Mythology begins again in the aftermath of catastrophe. What disappointed me most was how surprisingly "traditional" the staging turned out to be. What I mean by "traditional" is in its theatrical approach, which is essentially a straightforward exercise in illustrating an imagined setting. This cannot be described as "modern" because there is hardly any attempt to translate the mythology into a contemporary context, or a coherent symbolic language.

Coming from Serebrennikov, of all stage directors, this feels underwhelming. He is one of the directors I know who always fascinates audiences by formal experimentation, theatrical tricks and elaborate games with representation. Yet in *Das Rheingold*, he seems content simply to construct a spectacle out of his own imagination of what a mythical world might look like. The material offers so many possibilities, and he does remarkably little with them.

Even the use of video, a medium he has employed much more inventively elsewhere, feels disappointingly elementary. The opening prelude is perhaps the one sequence that almost works: Alberich running naked through a desolate landscape, the camera gradually rising as the stage curtain lifts. Perhaps these technical stuff work better in the almost miniature world of *Petrov's Flu*. Confronted with something as vast as the *Ring*, however, he seems to have lost his ability to organize the material. He might as well go back to films which are also becoming kind of boring in recent years.

Richard Wagner’s *Das Rheingold* opened the Salzburg Easter Festival’s new *Ring* cycle at the Felsenreitschule on 27 March 2026, with further performances on 1 and 6 April. Kirill Petrenko conducted the Berliner Philharmoniker. Christian Gerhaher sang Wotan, Leigh Melrose sang Alberich, Brenton Ryan sang Loge, and Catriona Morison sang Fricka. Yurii Karikh designed the video. [Festival programme](https://osterfestspiele.at/en/programme/2026/rheingold-2-2)
