A classic children's opera with unexpectedly conventional visual design. The fable's simplified narrative makes it accessible, showing that opera still knows how to attract children. Humperdinck's adaptation preserves all the qualities of the Brothers Grimm: a single narrative path, an unexpected adventure and a happy ending. His vivid use of folk melody is an enduring musical virtue. The poor family's struggle to survive, the gingerbread house's temptation and trap - these rather contrived symbols can still be reawakened in a modern context. The production cleverly gives stage and story a shared simplicity through flat, graphic settings. Restless lighting and ever-enlarged painted backdrops use skewed perspectives to probe the human desires projected by the scenery. Like the gingerbread house itself, it is bright and sugary on the outside, but filled with the coldness of industrial civilization within.
