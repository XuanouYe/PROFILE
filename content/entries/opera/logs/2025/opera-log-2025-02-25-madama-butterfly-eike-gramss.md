Cultural symbols create spatial opposition: Japanese screens stand beneath a huge American flag, while a moon and an eagle hang side by side in the air. The stage direction stays within tradition, conveying the dissonance of a colonial narrative. Foreign symbols are inserted into Japan’s closed structure, and an emotional tragedy unfolds within it. The aesthetic space remains confined to Puccini’s familiar emotional logic, but the writing is simply so beautiful: a lovely soprano, lyrical arias, and finely layered orchestral playing. Several times the stage lights dim while the strings continue, hypnotically delaying the destruction of Butterfly and of the colonized East Asian femininity she embodies.
